# Uncertainty of the astrometric calibration

Status: prototype (`RMS/Astrometry/AstrometryUncertainty.py`), not yet integrated in SkyFit2.

## Motivation

Neither the ECSV nor WMPL carries any information on how good the calibration is at a given point of the image.
WMPL estimates the trajectory uncertainty only from self-consistency:

- the per-station noise is the RMS of the angular residuals from the fitted line (`obs.ang_res_std`),
- the Monte Carlo adds this noise independently to every point,
- the ECSV reader only reads the positions (`datetime`, `azimuth`, `altitude`, `x_image`, `y_image`, `mag_data`).

A calibration error (pointing, rotation, scale, distortion) shifts all points of a station coherently. The track stays
straight, so `ang_res_std` does not see it, but the plane of that station rotates, and with it the radiant and the
heights. The goal is to estimate this error for every point, and later pass it to WMPL as a correlated term.

## Method

1. The free parameters are the ones used to map image points to the sky: pointing (`RA_d`, `dec_d`, `pos_angle_ref`,
   `F_scale` unless the scale is fixed) and the forward distortion (`x_poly_fwd`, plus `y_poly_fwd` for poly models).
2. The residuals are signed, 2 per star (tangent plane offsets along RA*cos(dec) and Dec) of the calibration stars
   stored in `platepar.star_list`. The fit itself minimizes unsigned separations, which cannot give a covariance.
3. The Jacobian is computed numerically with the same mapping used for the picks (`xyToRaDecPP`). The parameters
   span many orders of magnitude, so every step is scaled to move the positions by ~0.01 arcsec.
4. `cov = s^2 (J^T J)^-1`, with `s^2 = sum(r^2)/(2N - p)`, inverted via SVD of the column-normalized Jacobian.
5. The covariance is propagated to any pixel: `C_point = J_point cov J_point^T` (2x2 error ellipse on the sky).

The covariance is computed after the fit, independently of how the fit was done (the fit is staged, so the
Jacobian of any single stage does not give the joint covariance). It works with any existing platepar that has stars.

If the platepar is not at the least squares optimum of its own model, the residuals are not orthogonal to the
Jacobian and the script warns that the uncertainty is underestimated.

## Usage

```
python -m RMS.Astrometry.AstrometryUncertainty platepar.cal [--mc N] [--fixedscale] [--pointing]
```

- `--mc N` refits the platepar N times with `fitAstrometry` to synthetic stars with the measured noise and compares
  the empirical errors with the linear propagation.
- `--fixedscale`, `--pointing` must match the options used in the fit.

The map of the 1-sigma semi-major axis is saved next to the platepar as `*_uncertainty.png`.

## Validation

| Platepar | Stars | Star residual | Calibration 1-sigma min / median / max | Monte Carlo / linear (5-95%) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| NZ005H, radial7-odd | 129 | 30.6" | 3.9 / 6.8 / 251" | 1.02 (0.89-1.13) |
| NZ005J, radial7-odd | 66 | 112.7" | 22.6 / 36.9 / 617" | 1.03 (0.88-1.14) |
| NZ005H refitted as poly3+radial | 129 | 70.9" | 13.1 / 29.7 / 488" | 2.41 (1.11-5.68), warning |

- Radial models: the linear propagation matches the Monte Carlo refits. The uncertainty is smallest where the stars
  are dense and grows steeply in the corners without stars (extrapolation of the high order radial terms).
- Poly model: the RMS fit does not reach the optimum of its own model (67" sky RMS vs 36.7" for a joint forward fit
  of the same parameters). With a joint forward fit, Monte Carlo / linear drops to 1.09, so the propagation is right
  and the extra error comes from the fit. Likely cause: for poly models the pointing comes from the reverse fit and
  the forward distortion is fitted with the pointing fixed; radial models have a final joint step, poly models do
  not. Repeating `fitAstrometry` does not change it. This was seen on one platepar only (generated by refitting a
  radial one), it needs to be confirmed on real poly platepars.

## Limitations

- Assumes the distortion model is adequate. The Monte Carlo validates the propagation, not the model.
- Does not include the random error of each pick (centroid), which goes to WMPL separately.
- Far outside the stars (e.g. near the horizon) the linear approximation may fail.
- A calibration from another date, or a pointing-only fit, does not include possible camera motion.
- `--fixedscale` and `--pointing` run, but were not validated with Monte Carlo.

## Next steps

3. ECSV: export the per-point errors, and the correlated part (a few modes: 2 shifts, rotation, scale) so that
